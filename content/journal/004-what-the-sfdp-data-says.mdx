The Solana Foundation runs a delegation program that has handed stake to thousands
of validators since 2021. It publishes an API with every applicant in it. It does
not publish any analysis of that API. So we pulled all 10,967 records and looked.

We are not neutral. Vyra Labs is one of the 89 validators sitting in the queue,
waiting for a mainnet onboarding email. Anatoly has said the program is past its
time and Helius has argued for dismantling it, but neither argument had the numbers,
because nobody had counted them. We counted.

```text
Dataset        10,967 participant records
Detail         460 active, plus a 705-record sample of retired
Joined with    Stakewiz, 696 live validators
Snapshot       2 September 2026, epoch 1024
```

## "Retired" mostly means never got in

The FAQ explains retirement as graduation: a validator eligible on mainnet for 18
months or more, still under 5,000 SOL of independent stake, gets cycled out to make
room. The policy is "onboard 1, offboard 3."

Read the state counts and you would think 3,516 validators went through mainnet and
got cycled out. They did not. We sampled 705 retired records: only 17% carry an
onboarding epoch. The other 83% were never onboarded at all. They applied, got
approved onto testnet, and were dropped before any delegation arrived. That is
roughly 2,908 validators who never reached mainnet delegation.

- **Rejected, 6,991.** Application declined, plus operators removed for abuse.
- **Retired, 3,516.** About 608 graduated, the rest never reached mainnet.
- **Approved, 360.** Onboarded and holding a delegation. The live program.
- **TestnetOnboarded, 89.** On testnet, waiting for a mainnet email.
- **Pending, 11.** Applied, not yet decided.

We pulled the API twice, 48 hours apart. Exactly one record changed: a testnet
validator moved from `TestnetOnboarded` to `Retired`, with zero vote credits and no
recent bonus epochs. It had stopped running, and that forfeits your place in the
queue.

## Onboarding stopped in December 2025

We reconstructed the monthly onboarding rate from the `sfdp2OnboardingEpoch` field,
counting every validator that was ever onboarded, including those later retired. For two years it was steady: between 13 and 31 validators a month. Then
November 2025 saw 14, December saw 1, and it never recovered. 2026 is averaging 2.5 a
month. Yearly totals: about 188 in 2024, about 202 in 2025, 20 so far in 2026, a fall
of roughly 85% on a full-year run rate.

The Foundation's own February 2026 case study explains the timing. The final
transition began in October 2025, when stake matching stepped from 1:1 to 0.5:1, and
the onboarding data shows that landing about two months later. No announcement said
onboarding had halted. The pages are current, client versions are published four
epochs ahead, and applications are still open.

## The program worked

Every cohort it onboarded survived at a higher rate than the last.

```text
Cohort          Onboarded(est)   Still approved   Survival
Pre-Dec 2023    ~549             115              21%
2024            ~188             93               50%
2025            ~202             127              63%
2026             20              20               100%
```

Ignore 2026: nobody is old enough to hit the 18-month retirement mark, so the 100%
only reflects how recent they are. Across pre-2024, 2024 and 2025 the direction is
steady. Every time the Foundation tightened its criteria, the validators it let in
were more likely to become self-sustaining, which is what the program set out to do.

## The ones still in it barely need it

The typical survivor now holds more stake from the open market (median 81,063 SOL)
than from the Foundation (median 69,646 SOL). Only 4 of 356 sit near the 5,000 SOL
retirement threshold. The Foundation's share of network stake fell from 44.4% at
launch to 5.45%, while the stake on the validators it helped build rose to 15.2% of
the network.

They also run clean: median skip rate 0.00%, 354 of 356 run Jito, one is delinquent.
If the program ended tomorrow, this group would keep running on the stake it has
already attracted.

## Concentration, and the sybil claim

By country, the set concentrates: Germany 91, United States 65, Netherlands 48,
Singapore 40, Japan 32. A program built for decentralisation produced a validator set
that is 26% German by count. Within SFDP, stake clusters on a few hosts:

```text
SFDP host      Validators   Share of SFDP stake
Allnodes       48           16.4%
Latitude.sh    59           12.4%
Vultr          38            7.6%
```

The top three hold 36% of SFDP stake, and the 25% ASN and 15% data centre limits apply
per validator, so they cap where one node sits without constraining the aggregate. The largest single SFDP cluster is 32
validators in Frankfurt on one ASN and client version, at 9.6% of program stake.

Zoom out to all of mainnet, grouped by ASN (since provider names in the metadata are
inconsistent), and it is far starker:

```text
Mainnet stake by ASN (Stakewiz, epoch 1024)
ASN        Provider                Active   Stake    Share
AS20326    TeraSwitch               94     112.0M    25.5%
AS396356   Latitude.sh              94      60.3M    13.8%
AS395201   Allnodes                 73      31.0M     7.1%
AS7979     Servers.com              21      20.8M     4.7%
AS16125    Dedicated Servers (LT)   18      19.8M     4.5%
```

Three networks carry 46.4% of all active Solana stake. TeraSwitch alone runs 106
validators, 94 active and 12 delinquent, holding a quarter of the network. That is the
number behind the 12 August route leak, when a bad route out of its Miami site pushed
roughly 29% of stake delinquent, close to the one-third of stake that would halt the
network.
(validators.app files TeraSwitch higher, at 130.3M SOL, some of it under "Private
Customer"; both sources put it near or above a quarter.)

And not one of the 356 SFDP validators sits on TeraSwitch. It is the only ASN above the
program's 25% concentration cap, at 25.5%, so hosting there fails the residual criteria
and costs you the delegation. The rule that shields the program from the largest host
is what pushes every SFDP validator onto the next tier. (Disclosure: our own testnet
node runs on TeraSwitch, which is exactly why our mainnet box will land elsewhere,
likely Latitude, once onboarding comes.)

On 24 August 2026, <a href="https://x.com/andreivacariu_" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">@andreivacariu\_</a> published research claiming about one in five active SFDP
validators are run by three coordinated operators, citing 40 of 107 nodes in one pool
sharing a host, version, stake sources, and governance vote timing. Public data
confirms the clustering; the Frankfurt group is exactly that signature. Clustering is
not proof of control, since one host serves many independent operators. The
response, though, is on the record: three days later, the six validators in the smaller
cluster were rejected. That shows the Rejected bucket is an active enforcement tool.
The Foundation used it to remove six operators who were already inside the program.

## The delegation never covered the cost

At 5.17% APY and 5% commission, the median delegation earns the operator about 180
SOL a year, roughly $18,600 at $103. Mainnet voting costs about 1.08 SOL a day, 394
SOL a year, roughly $40,700. Break-even is around 152,500 SOL, more than double the
median delegation. After Alpenglow, at our measured 0.80 SOL a day, it falls to about
113,000.

So the delegation was never meant to be a business. It covers about a year of costs
while a validator finds real stake, which is exactly what the retirement rule then
tests for. And 126 of 356 run 0% commission: for a third of the program, the validator
brings users to another product and earns nothing itself.

## Who gets in now, and who waits

Twenty validators onboarded in 2026, including Step Finance, Raiku, and Tapedrive
(onboarded after just nine epochs on testnet). Median
time on testnet before onboarding was 22 epochs, about six weeks. Meanwhile the queue
has 89 waiting, median tenure 318 epochs (roughly 21 months); the longest has waited
509 epochs, about 34 months, with a flawless record. None of 2026's twenty came from
that long tail. On the evidence, a named contribution to the ecosystem counts for more
than time in the queue or a clean record.

## What this data cannot tell you

- **Epoch 577 is a migration stamp, not a cohort.** 57% of onboarding records share
  that December 2023 epoch, when v2 tracking began and every existing participant was
  stamped at once. Month-by-month analysis uses post-migration records only.
- **The retired figures are a 20% sample**, 705 of 3,516, scaled by 4.99. Cohort
  counts carry roughly 10% sampling error. Read them as estimates.
- **Clustering is not proof of control, and nothing here reveals intent.** The
  onboarding collapse is a measured fact; whether it is a deliberate halt, a backlog,
  or a staffing change (the Staking Operations Lead changed around April 2026) is not
  something this data answers.

## Method

All 10,967 records pulled from the Foundation's public participant API
(`api.solana.org/api/validators`) on 2 September 2026, with detail records for all
460 active participants and a 705-record random sample of retired. Onboarding history
from the `sfdp2OnboardingEpoch` field; stake, hosting, client and geography joined
against Stakewiz, which also supplies the network-wide stake by ASN, cross-checked
against validators.app. Vote cost figures are our own measurements.
